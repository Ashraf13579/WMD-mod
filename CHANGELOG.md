## 1.2.0 (14 Nov 2026) - RELEASED (GitHub v1.2.0): Air Battles (ship propulsion and air battles need Sable)
- New TOW BAR: right-click an aircraft's nose landing gear to hook up, then walk backwards to pull it or forwards to push it (up to 3 blocks per second, any size of aircraft). The nose wheel turns to follow you, the parking brake lets go while you tow and sets again when you let go. The bar telescopes to how far away you stood when you hooked up. Works on the ground, on a carrier deck and on a deck lift. It comes off when you use it again, put it away, walk too far, the aircraft takes off or the pilot uses the throttle. Only one person can tow an aircraft at a time, and only someone allowed to use it. Recipe: steel plate, aluminium plate and an iron ingot on a diagonal.
- REVERSE TAXI: in the cockpit, on the wheels at idle throttle, hold Left Ctrl to roll the aircraft backwards with an electric motor in the nose wheel (2.5 blocks per second). Q / E steer it. The key can be changed in the controls.
- New HANGAR TURNTABLE: lay turntable tiles in a square in your floor (7 x 7 up to 31 x 31) and the round plate inside it turns a parked aircraft so it faces the hangar door, the catapult or the runway. Park with every wheel on the plate and the throttle at idle, then right-click a tile: turn the plate -90, 180 or +90 degrees, or turn the aircraft's nose to north, east, south or west. Redstone at the corner tile repeats the last order, and the Flight Deck Console can turn it too.
- The turntable takes the look of any block you right-click onto it, so it blends into your floor; only a thin steel ring, four blue heading lights and a blue nose arrow show. It works on a ship's deck as well, needs power from a busbar under its corner tile, and you, mobs and items standing on it turn with it.
- The turntable checks before it turns: it won't start if a wheel is off the plate, the aircraft isn't on all its wheels, the throttle is up, or a wing would hit a wall or another aircraft while turning. While it turns, the aircraft's wheels are chocked and held to their spot on the plate, so it can't creep off; if the aircraft still stops following the plate, the turn stops right there instead of throwing it off.
- Flight Deck Console: each turntable has one Turn button; it opens a small chooser (-90, 180, +90 degrees, or point the aircraft's nose north, east, south or west).
- Fixed: the Flight Deck Console could list one deck lift (or turntable) many times with smaller sizes when it had been built by a command or structure; each one now shows once.
- Storage Tank rebuilt as a glass tank: you can see the stored liquid inside, in its own colour, rising and falling with the level. The surface ripples and slowly swirls; while the tank fills, a stream pours from the fill pipe inside with splashes, and while it empties the liquid swirls faster over the drain. The spiral stairs are gone. Same size, same pipe connections, same 256 buckets.
- The Crude Storage Tank is removed: the Storage Tank holds crude oil just as well. Crude tanks already placed in your worlds (and crude tank items) turn into Storage Tanks by themselves, with their oil and pipes kept.
- Storage Tank recipe: steel, tin and glass (no aluminium any more), so it is an early-game tank like the old crude tank.
- Tanks can now be shot up and blown up. Missiles, bullets and any explosion damage a Storage Tank, an aircraft's fuel tanks, drop tanks and power core. The glass Storage Tank cracks from the first hit (the cracks spread as it takes more), then leaks through them (you see the liquid squirting out), catches fire if what is inside burns, and finally the glass shatters and the tank fails. A leaking glass tank shows a real hole in the glass with shards on its base, and soot once it has burned. Drop tanks show dents and bullet holes once hit, and turn scorched with a peeled panel while they burn. What happens depends on the liquid: jet fuel, gasoline and other light fuels explode in a fireball and leave a burning pool; LPG and hydrogen go up in a huge fireball; crude oil and heavy oils burn for a long time under a black smoke column (crude can boil over in a sudden burst of fire); steam bursts out scalding hot; chlorine and titanium tetrachloride release a poison cloud; water and brine just splash out. Explosions can set off the tanks next to them. On an aircraft a hit fuel tank streams fuel vapour, then burns hard and explodes; drop a burning drop tank to save the plane (it blows up where it lands). An Engine Repair Kit fixes a damaged tank (aircraft at rest). Explosions follow the server's explosion and land-claim settings. New server option explosions.tankDamage (on by default) turns all of this off. While it is on, explosions no longer tear a Storage Tank away in one go: they damage it until it fails.
- Fixed: the Interceptor Launcher was see-through along the seams of its missile pack (you could see the sky through it). It is solid now.
- Pumpjack: the whole front 3x3 (the wellhead end, where the rod goes into the ground) now counts. It pumps if oil lies under any of those 9 blocks, so you no longer have to hit the deposit with one exact block. It can't be placed without oil under its front 3x3, and the screen and status say so.

  - Rising Bottom Mine (3 x 2 x 3): lies on the seabed. Load it with a Torpedo T1; when a ship passes over it (up to 24 blocks above), its cap flips open and a rocket rises and blows up at the hull. Load another T1 to use it again.
  - Captor Mine: a moored canister you load with a Torpedo T2. When an enemy submarine comes within 48 blocks, the homing torpedo leaves the canister and goes after it.
  - A sonar ping shows mines as contacts (you can pick one as a torpedo target), and a torpedo or depth charge going off close to a mine sets it off too. Sneak with an empty hand to take your mine back.
  - Lay mines from a ship: use a mine at the rail of a ship (or from a pier or a boat) and it goes over the side, splashes, sinks to the seabed and moors itself, its cable paying out until the mine floats at its depth. Sneak + use the mine in your hand to set that depth (3 to 90 blocks). A Rising Bottom Mine or a Captor Mine takes its torpedo from your inventory on the way down, so it lands loaded. You need at least 8 blocks of water, and you have to stand at the rail (not over your own deck). If it can't moor where it lands (land, someone's claim, no room), you get it back.
- K-Gun (Depth Charge Thrower, 1 x 2 x 2): throws a depth charge about 16 blocks out over the side, the way you were looking when you placed it. Fire it from its screen, with a redstone pulse, or with SALVO on a fire control console on its frequency. You see the drum fly off its arbor, the barrel kick back in a puff of smoke, and the davit swing the spare drum onto the barrel. A depth charge rack placed against it keeps it supplied. The depth charge leaves on its arbor (the cradle it sits on): the two part in the air and the arbor tumbles into the sea with its own splash; when the K-gun reloads, a fresh arbor drops into the barrel and the davit sets the next charge on it.

- Towed Array Winch (2 x 2 x 2): put it in your submarine's stern with the fairlead facing back. Powered, it streams a long sonar array behind the boat that follows your wake and hears ships 3,000 blocks away. It hears nothing while it streams out, while you go fast or for a while after a hard turn: run straight and slow to listen.
- Listening Node (2 x 3 x 2): a wireless sonar post for the seabed or a pier. No cables: it reports to your sonar consoles on its frequency and runs on its own battery; its little current turbine recharges it in water (for ever in flowing water).
- Coastal Battery (4 x 3 x 4): a shore anti-ship launcher with four cruise missiles. It turns to the target your fire control console picked (or a typed X Z), raises its canisters, blows the covers and fires missiles that skim the sea and home on the ship. Submerged submarines can't be hit.
- Dock Crane (3 x 6 x 3): loads your submarine at the pen. Put torpedoes or missiles in a chest or torpedo rack on the pier within the crane's reach and it swings, lowers its hook and loads them into torpedo tubes, launch cells, the coastal battery or the submarine's own torpedo rack.
- Manganese nodules: fields of metal lumps on the floor of deep oceans (new chunks only). You cannot mine them by hand: bring them up with a Dredge Winch or a Seabed Crawler. Turn them off with the `disabledOreGen` option ("nodules").
- Dredge Winch (2 x 3 x 2): place it on a pier or a ship looking out over the water. Its A-frame swings out and drops a grab bucket on a cable to the seabed; it grabs a nodule field within a few blocks and brings up 2-3 nodules. Slow and cheap: the early way. AUTO repeats it, DROP ONE or a redstone pulse does one.
- Collector Rig (5 x 5 x 5) + Seabed Crawler: stand the rig on the edge of a deck or pier, put a crawler in and press LAUNCH. The crawler goes down on its riser hose, finds the nodule fields within 24 blocks and drives from one to the next collecting them; the nodules are pumped up into the rig, which separates them into manganese and nickel-cobalt concentrate. RECALL brings the crawler home.
- Nodules smelt into manganese; the rig's concentrates give more manganese plus nickel-cobalt alloy. The T4 supercavitating torpedo now needs 2 nickel-cobalt alloy (instead of 2 titanium), and the crawler's tracks need manganese.
- Nodule survey: the Sonar Console has a new SEABED tab. Press SURVEY SEABED and every sonar dome on its frequency pings the seabed and maps the
  manganese nodule fields within 256 blocks: they show on the console's map at their real size and in a list (how many nodule blocks are left,
  how far, which way, how deep). A survey costs as much as a ping, shares its cooldown and every enemy sonar hears it. The marks stay for
  about an hour; the Dredge Winch and Collector Rig screens show your nearest surveyed field. A deep submarine without its comms buoy only
  sees its own surveys.
- Drive Coupling (needs Create): a WMD electric drive that turns power into Create rotation, up to 32,768 SU at 2,500 FE/t. Put it against the naval reactor's drive flange (east side) and it takes the power straight from the reactor, or feed it with a busbar. Right-click picks 16 / 32 / 64 / 128 RPM, sneak + right-click reverses. One reactor can now drive a submarine's propellers. Without Create the block simply does not exist.
- Naval Reactor (3 x 3 x 3): a compact submarine power plant, 50,000 FE/s at full power. Load fuel rods at the front (a rod lasts 4 hours at full power) and take the spent rods to the reprocessing plant. Its glowing core sits in a round glass coolant tank that you can watch fill up: the sea it touches (a submerged sub fills it for free) or water piped in. Under a quarter full it starts to overheat. A nearby blast can crack the glass: the tank leaks, hisses and sprays until you seal it with a reactor repair kit. The light ring and core change colour with its state (blue running, red chasing lights on an alarm, the core turning orange to white-hot). QUIET mode (30 %) stops the pumps so enemy sonar cannot hear it; above that the pumps hum. Lose the cooling and it SCRAMs itself; you then have about 25 seconds to restore water before a small meltdown. EMP freezes it.
- Hull-integrated tops: the Periscope's top, the Escape Pod's and the Communications Buoy's hatch plate and the Towed Array Winch's back face are now part of your submarine's hull: build the hull with a hole and the block fills it. Those cells are solid and stay dry (they keep the water out) and the mast, pod, buoy or cable reaches the sea through its own gland or hatch. They only open when the outside is open water or air ("blocked" otherwise); nothing passes through other blocks any more.
- Periscope (1 x 3 x 1): raise the mast and right-click to look through it from above the waves (turn all round, scroll for x1.5 / x6). Only works at periscope depth. While it is up, radar sees a tiny blip and the mast leaves a wake.
- Communications Buoy (2 x 2 x 2) and radio under water: a submarine more than 10 blocks deep can no longer talk to anything outside its own hull: your shore consoles lose its tubes and sonar, and its consoles lose yours. Release the buoy (64-block cable) to float it on the surface and link up again; radar and sonar can spot a floating buoy. Server option `navalDeepLinkLoss` turns the rule off.
- Anti-Torpedo Torpedo: made on the torpedo line, loaded in a tube or deck launcher. When your sonar hears an enemy torpedo heading for you, the launcher fires it by itself; it chases the torpedo and blows it up.
- Escape Pod (2 x 3 x 2): up to 4 players standing next to it ride it to the surface, at the press of EJECT or by itself when the submarine implodes or floods. Re-arm it with an Escape Capsule.
