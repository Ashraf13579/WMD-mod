# WMD Changelog

## 1.1.0 (9 Nov 2026) - RELEASED (GitHub v1.1.0): the deep-sea update

### Sound
- All machine sounds now follow one standard. A machine's size decides how loud it is and how far you hear it: small devices about 12 blocks, normal machines about 14, heavy industry about 15, the fusion reactor and the collider further. Before, some machines (crusher, arc furnace, pumpjack, diesel generator and others) could be heard at full loudness from anywhere nearby, and others were almost silent.
- Every alarm and siren now carries the same distance (64 blocks). Doors, hatches, clicks and releases are now consistent with each other too.

### Machine animations
- Robot dock: the charge arm is now a sprung arm with two contact shoes. When your missile robot parks, the arm comes down onto its charge port, the contact closes with a clunk and a spark, and only then does charging start. A bar on the arch post fills with the robot's battery, light strips along the pad run toward the arch while the robot drives home, and a lamp stack shows green (ready / full), amber (robot coming home or charging) or red (no power). The arm lifts with a click when the robot leaves. Fixed: the "raised" arm used to hang down toward the robot.
- Nitration reactor: one batch now plays out in front of you. A rotary feeder under the hopper turns in three steps to measure the nitrate dust in, the red valve wheel on the naphtha line turns open and shut, and a gauge glass on the front shows the mix rising. While it reacts, a cooling coil round the bottom carries the heat to a small fan cooler at the back (its fan spins up and coasts down), and reddish-brown fumes rise from a new vent stack. At the end of every batch a slide gate under the tank opens with a clunk, the freshly cast HE charge drops onto an open trough and slides into the output, and the level in the glass falls back. A green / amber / red lamp stack shows running, dosing or discharging, and waiting for input or a full output.
- Deck torpedo launcher: each of the three tubes now has its own hinged muzzle cap and breech door. When the launcher is ready, the cap of the tube that fires next swings open with a clunk and you can see the torpedo nose inside. On firing, that tube kicks back in its straps, a puff of air bursts from the muzzle as the torpedo is thrown over the side, and the cap closes again over the empty tube. While a torpedo rack reloads a tube, its breech door stands open. The mount now turns on a visible gear ring driven by a small motor and pinion, and it speeds up and slows down smoothly instead of moving at one speed. Four lamps on each breech show that tube's state: blue empty, green loaded, amber ready, red just fired. Torpedoes now leave from the tube they were loaded in.
- Magnesium cell: a small tapping crane now works the cell. At the end of each batch it lifts its crucible off the casting stand, swings it over the glowing metal well, dips it in, lifts it out glowing with molten magnesium, swings back and sets it down with a clunk just as the ingot arrives in the output. The peepholes and the metal well now brighten when the power comes on and slowly cool down when it stops, and faint chlorine wisps rise round the anodes.
- Early-warning array: the radar face now shows what it is doing. When it powers up the six panels light one by one; while it watches, a bright beam sweeps across the face row by row; when it spots a new launch or airship the whole face flashes twice, the red status light blinks and you hear a contact blip; while the missile is in the air the panel on the side it came from pulses (amber light) while the sweep goes on over the rest. The cooling fans on the roof spin while it runs and the red light on top blinks.
- Steam turbine: the front of the high-pressure casing is now cut open so you can watch its bladed rotor spin, next to the open low-pressure rotor. A flyball governor stands at the front: its balls swing out and rise as the turbine speeds up. The two steam valves have visible stems that lift with the steam flow. Three lamps on the console show its state: green running, blue ready (steam waiting but the power or spent steam has nowhere to go: a small turning gear keeps the rotor creeping round), red blinking = no steam. On start the valves pop open with a clunk and a puff of steam and the rotor spins up over a few seconds; on stop they slam shut with a hiss and the rotor coasts down, its whine dropping with it.
- Delayed coker: it now decokes its drums while it runs. One drum fills (its level glass rises) while the other is emptied: the top hatch slides open, the drill stem drops into the drum on its derrick and turns, chunks of coke tumble down the chute to the output, then the stem comes up, the hatch closes and the switch valve swaps the drums over with a heavy clunk. Each drum now has its own derrick, level glass and lamps (amber = filling, blue = being emptied); everything eases to a stop when the coker stops and picks up where it left off.
- Command center map table: the four projector posts now light up one after another when the room comes online and the hologram fades in with them; while it runs a short flicker passes round the posts. They go dark when the room has no power.
- Drone hive: a drone that lands keeps its rotors turning and they wind down while its bay lid closes.
- Chlor-alkali cell: gas bubbles fizz up the front of the cells while it runs, more the harder it works.
- Sonar dome: every active ping now sends a light sweep over the dome and a ring out into the water; the ping ring then blinks once for every ship the ping found.
- Reprocessing plant: the window now looks into the hot cell. While it works, two remote-handling tongs pass a spent fuel rod from the rack to a stand and on into the dissolver funnel, where it drops in with a flash, a clunk and a puff of steam (breeder rods for tritium show copper). The operator arms outside move along with the tongs, and the pulsed columns pump (turning cam, bellows, a float bobbing in the sight glass). Lamps by state: green working, amber powered and waiting, red blocked (output full); everything dark without power. It all eases in on start and glides back to rest on stop.
- Batteries: the charge bar now shows which way the energy flows: the next light blinks while charging, the top light flickers while discharging. The fans on the large batteries spin faster the more power goes through.
- Kroll reactor: the furnace now works like a real pit furnace. The heater windows glow up slowly as it heats and fade over about 20 seconds after it stops; the titanium tetrachloride feed valve turns open and you can see it drip in a small sight glass on the feed line; at the end of every batch the feed shuts, two hydraulic jacks lift the retort lid with a puff of fumes, the glowing titanium sponge shows inside, and the lid drops back onto its seat just as the sponge reaches the output. A new amber lamp shows when it is heating, cooling or open.
- Collector rig: the deck rig now shows the whole job. The riser winch speeds up and slows down smoothly as it pays out and hauls in, with a carriage sliding back and forth to lay the hose; the lift pump's shaft turns in an open frame, its motor hums and a pressure gauge pulses; you can see the slurry swirl through a glass band in the hydrocyclone; muddy water pours over the side into the sea. Every batch of nodules that comes up the riser lands with a rattle: the pump kicks, nodules spill into the now open hopper (the pile grows and shrinks with its contents) and a gush of mud goes over the side. A flap opens on the hopper for each batch of concentrate. New light stack: green working, amber hopper full or the field worked out, blinking red for a fault; the cabin window glows blue while it has power.
- Counter-battery radar: the radar now sets up like the real thing. Without power its antenna lies folded flat on top; when power arrives two hydraulic jacks push it upright, it locks with a clunk, then turns to face the way the radar's front points and keeps watching that sector (it still sees shells all round). When it traces an enemy shell it pings, its face lights flash twice and it swings onto the enemy gun with a red light, then returns to its sector. Cut the power and it turns back to the front and folds down again. The cooling fans spin, a small generator now runs beside the shelter, and a light stack shows blue folded, green watching, red tracking.
- Antimatter collider: the ring now shows the beam going round. A short violet comet of floor and beam-monitor lights chases the bunch round the ring every 2 seconds and each magnet's blue coil lamps light up as it passes. Every 16 seconds the ring slows the beam down: all the magnet lamps blink three times together and white cold vapour bursts out of the four new cryo vent stacks on the corner cabinets with a hiss. The five lamps over the hall door are now a progress meter for the antimatter cell (one more lights with a small flash every 4 minutes; all five stay lit while a finished cell waits inside).
- Command center building: the building now looks alive from outside. A radar bar turns on the front of the roof (slow in peace, faster at DEFCON 3-2, fastest at DEFCON 1) and runs down smoothly when the power goes. A small satellite dish on the back of the roof stays folded up until your command center controls satellites, then it swings out and slowly tracks them. Two thin radio masts on the back carry blinking red aviation lights, and three air-conditioning units on the back wall turn their fans faster the more modules are online. The blue light strips on the fins, the roof rim and the entrance canopy now show the DEFCON level in the same colours as the DEFCON tab: blue at 5, cyan at 4, yellow at 3, orange at 2 and red at 1 (red strobes with the ceiling lights); while the blast door is sealed they blink. Everything goes dark and stops under an EMP. The door works as before.
- Roaster: the kiln now turns like a real rotary kiln: the drum rolls slowly on its riding rings (about one turn every 10 seconds instead of a fast spin), speeds up gently when it starts and coasts to a stop. The rollers under the rings turn with it, the drive at the back is now open so you can see the small gear driving the big ring gear, and the coupling on the motor spins. The front window of the burner end shows the fire: a pilot flame lights with a clunk and a flash, then the flame grows and flickers with the gas flow, and glowing lumps of roasted ore tumble off the drum onto a glowing bed that stays hot for a while after it stops. The feed bin on top is now open and you can see the ore pile go down as it is used.
- Flare stack power plant: the flame at the top now shows how much gas it burns. It grows and shrinks with the power your grid takes (small at a trickle, a tall licking flame at full load, visible from much further away); with gas but no power demand only three small pilot flames flicker and an amber lamp blinks. The stack has a new steam ring round the tip: above a quarter load white steam jets into the flame and the black smoke clears (a valve at the foot of the stack opens with it). The generator set works visibly: a radiator fan spins up, the exhaust cap pops open and flutters, and the flame lights with a whoomp and a puff of black smoke. The flames have no hitbox.
- Vertical launch cell: a launch now plays out step by step. Four locking dogs round the hatch pull back first, then the hatch swings open; under it the white seal is whole while a round is loaded. At the launch the seal bursts (white flakes fly up), a cloud of gas (a bubble column under water) shoots out of the tube, and down at the gas generator the red firing wheel spins, the pressure needle jumps and a vent cap pops open with a hiss of steam. The hatch then closes and the dogs lock it again with a clack; the torn seal stays until you load the next round. A new lamp panel shows the cell's state: blue empty, green loaded, amber armed (blinks while the hatch opens), red just fired.
- Vertical launch cell fix: if the round, its capsule or the target disappeared while the hatch was opening, the hatch stayed open forever; it now closes again.
- XL dock: parking the XL transporter is now a small show. While the XL drives home, a chain of guide lights runs along both edges of the pad towards the parking spot and a green bar lights at the entrance. When it stops, the lights flash twice, then eight chocks flip up against the ends of its four tracks, one corner after another with a clack each; the charge arm swings out from the post over the XL's charge port and the contact drops onto it with a clunk and a spark. A new charge bar on the post shows the XL's battery (the next segment blinks while it charges) and three lamps on the front pillar show blue while the XL moves, amber while it charges and green when ready. When a job comes in, the XL waits a moment while the contact lifts, the arm folds back and the chocks drop, then rolls off as the lights run out to the front. All lights go dark without power.
- XL dock fix: the charge arm used to cut through the parked XL's deck; it now runs above it.
- Crude furnace: the lower door is now a sight window onto the burners. Short blue flames light when it starts firing and taller, livelier flames join as it heats up (fewer when it is fed slowly); they flicker all the time. When the crude runs out the flames go out at once, but the brick keeps glowing and cools down over about half a minute while a new amber lamp shows it is still hot. The top of the stack is open now with a damper inside: its lever swings open as the furnace heats up and closes again as it cools. A thin plume and heat shimmer rise from the stack top.
- Depth charge rack: a release now works like a real stern rack. A red lamp blinks, the trap at the low end drops open like a ramp while a stop rises behind the next charge, the first charge rolls down over the trap and off the stern (the falling charge carries on from exactly there), the trap clanks shut and the other charges roll down one place each. The charges on the rails always match what is loaded; DROP ONE and PATTERN work as before.
- Gas turbine: it now starts up like a real one. When it starts you hear the new starter motor under the intake whine up and see its fan spin while the shaft turns slowly; after a couple of seconds it lights with a whoosh, the combustor windows flash and a dark puff comes out of the stack, then the roar rises as it spools up and the starter winds down. Look into the intake: a ring of guide vanes is shut while it is stopped and opens while it runs, wider the more of its power is being used (it closes again as its storage fills up), with an outside ring and a blue actuator moving along. Hot air shimmers over the stack. When it stops the flame goes out, the vanes close and the rotor coasts down while the roar fades. New lamps: green running, blue blinking while starting (steady = waiting because the grid is full), red blinking = no fuel or no blades. The gauge now shows the turbine speed.
- Missile teleporter: the crane boom now slides out between two guide rails on top of the tower, so it no longer hangs in the air when it reaches out over the front.
- Solar mirror module: the glare shutters outside the side windows now swing on real hinges and close flat over the windows while it burns (they used to fly off the hull); the focus mast now stays in its sleeve when fully out.
- Assembly bay: the loader's clamshell grab now slides open and shut on guide rods instead of its halves hanging in the air.
- Station laser: the capacitor drum lids now rise on a stem when it arms instead of floating above the drums.

### Deep sea
- Noisemaker Launcher: a sealed hull block for submarines (water never gets in). Build it into the hull from inside, facing the sea; it holds 4 noisemakers and fires one out of its muzzle on FIRE, a redstone pulse, or AUTO: when a stranger's torpedo comes within 48 blocks of your ship it fires a decoy by itself (one at a time, never at your own or your friends' torpedoes). Needs a busbar on its inside face (800 FE a shot).
- Noisemakers now have a model: a small canister that hangs upright in the water with bubble jets while it sounds, then sinks away (it used to be bubbles only).
- K-gun mine laying: the K-gun now also throws sea mines. Put a mine on it by hand (or in its screen); a rising bottom mine or a captor mine takes its T1 / T2 torpedo from your inventory as it goes in (or right-click the K-gun with one later). THROW or a redstone pulse lays it about 16 blocks out, where it sinks and moors itself like a hand-laid mine, at the depth set on the K-gun's dial (3 to 90). A fire control SALVO skips a K-gun that has a mine loaded. Taking the mine out or breaking the K-gun gives the torpedo back.
- Sonar console HULL page: a new HULL tab shows the submarine the console is in (needs Create: Deep Seas): how deep you are against your crush depth, sealed or breached, cracks, how much water is in the bow, middle and stern, and where the leaks are. The status turns red when the hull is breached or past 90 % of the crush depth; the console's small screen shows it too.
- Hull Penetrator: a sealed busbar in a solid, watertight block. Build it into your submarine's hull to carry power to equipment outside; it counts as hull (rated 400 deep with Create: Deep Seas) and its light shows when power flows.
- Torpedo Net: a heavy steel ring net with a head rope, round buoys that bob on the surface, a foot chain with sinkers and anchored end posts. Hold the net item and click one corner in the water, then the opposite corner (both in one upright line: the same X or the same Z); a frame shows the net, its size and how many nets it costs before you place it (sneak-click cancels). Up to 32 long and 16 deep, 1 net item per 4 blocks of net, only in water, one layer only (no other net within 3 blocks in front or behind). T1, T2 and T3 torpedoes blow up on it and tear a hole; a ship more than 4 blocks behind the net takes no damage. The T4 rips straight through and leaves a smaller hole. Break any part to roll the whole net up: you get the nets back for the parts that are not torn. Nets placed in a test world before this version turn back into water and give their item back.
- Balance: torpedoes can now hole the toughest hulls. Armour plate, obsidian, netherite and other blast-proof blocks used to stop every torpedo, so a sub built from them could only be sunk by crushing it deep. Now tough hulls just get smaller holes: obsidian or netherite takes about a third of an iron-hull hole from a T1, WMD armour plate is barely scratched by a T1 but a T3 opens about a third and a T4 about two thirds. Depth charges still don't hole these hulls; they sink subs by crushing them at depth.
- Deep-rated hulls (needs Create: Deep Seas): Manganese-Steel Hull Plate, Superalloy Deep Hull and the see-through Deep Viewport, made from the seabed nodule metals. Deep Seas cracks a submarine when it dives below the rating of its weakest outer block (glass 28, iron 101): these hold to 200, 400 and 200 blocks, and WMD's own torpedo tubes, launch cells, periscope, sonar, escape pod and buoy now hold to 400 so they are never the weak spot. A sub whose whole outer hull is rated this high also resists WMD hits: a torpedo or depth charge implodes it only 10 (manganese) or 20 (superalloy) blocks deeper than a steel sub; the T4 still kills at any depth. New server option `deepHullRatings` (off = Deep Seas' own hull numbers).
- Anechoic Tile: matte black rubber cladding for your submarine (seabed manganese, steel plate and black wool). Clad the outside of the hull: the more of the outer hull it covers, the quieter the boat (up to 15 dB, so enemy sonar hears you much closer in) and the closer an enemy sonar ping or torpedo seeker has to be to find you (up to 40 % closer). Only tiles on the outside count; with Create: Deep Seas a tile holds to 150 blocks deep.
- Deep Sonar Upgrade (needs nickel-cobalt alloy from the seabed): right-click a Sonar Dome or Hydrophone Array with it. The dome then listens out to 3,000 blocks (was 2,000), the array to 6,000 (was 4,000), and positions worked out from two sonars are twice as exact. A cyan light on the sonar screen shows it is fitted; take it out again in the Upgrades drawer, and breaking the sonar drops it.
- Torpedoes T1, T2 and T3: they run under water, hold their depth, arm after 15 blocks and go off when they hit a ship, pass under its keel, or reach the end of their run. A hit punches holes in a hull, and a hit on a deep submarine can make it implode. A running torpedo leaves a bubble wake from its propellers and, when it runs shallow, a track on the surface. Homing (T2) and wire guidance (T3) come later; for now they run straight.
- Torpedo Tube (1 x 1 x 4): build it through your submarine's hull; it points the way you look when you place it. Load it at the breech, set the run depth and press FIRE (or use a redstone pulse).
- Deck Torpedo Launcher (2 x 2 x 3): three tubes for boats and piers; set a bearing, it turns and drops a torpedo over the side.
- Torpedo Rack (2 x 2 x 4): holds 4 torpedoes (you can see them in the cradles) and reloads a tube or deck launcher next to it by itself.
- Torpedo Line: three modules (section shop, fit-out bay, test stand) that make torpedoes: the hull is rolled and cut, robot arms fit the battery, motor, warhead and seeker, the torpedo is joined, tested in a water tank and put in the test stand's rack of 4. Place the section shop, then build the other two from its Line tab. Torpedoes are made only on the line.
- Submarines from Create: Deep Seas (optional): WMD now knows when a ship is SUBMERGED. Radar, recon and the command center no longer see a
  submerged submarine (a surfaced one is still a normal target). New server options: `navalSubHoles` (blasts blow holes in submerged hulls),
  `navalSubImplosion` (a heavy hit can implode a Deep Seas submarine; the deeper it is, the smaller the blast needed) and
  `navalRadarSeesSubmerged` (off by default).
- Sonar: the Sonar Dome (goes through a hull: listens and can PING), the Hydrophone Array (a long-range passive listening station for the
  seabed or a pier) and the Sonar Console (a waterfall display, contacts, positions and PING ALL). Moving ships are loud, slow ones run
  silent; two sonars on one frequency hearing the same ship give its position; a ping finds every ship near you but everyone hears it. Sonar only hears and finds ships that are in the water: flying ships are radar's job. A ship that stops makes no sound at all; only a ping finds it. A new ping replaces the old ping results. The broken pieces of a destroyed submarine show as one wreck.
- Blasts under water now damage ship hulls (water used to swallow the whole blast, like TNT under water): holes in a submerged submarine let the sea in, and a hit below a ship's waterline breaks the hull there too. A torpedo tears a hole about 6 to 8 blocks across (bigger for higher tiers).
  New field manual chapter "Deep sea".
- Busbars can now be placed under water: they stay waterlogged, so you can run power along the seabed, under piers and inside submarines.
- Machines, pipes, valves, item ducts, sorters, batteries, alarms, launch terminals and supply interfaces placed in water now stay in water (waterlogged) instead of sitting in an air bubble. Blocks placed before this stay dry until you break and place them again.
- Torpedo Fire Control Console (2 x 2 x 1, a wall console like the sonar console): set your tubes and deck launchers to its frequency and they all show up in one list with their torpedo, depth and gyro. Pick a target that your sonar found (a ping result or a spot two sonars cross on), press AIM ALL and every launcher gets its gyro angle, bearing and run depth for that target (under a ship's keel, or at a submarine's depth). Then FIRE one or fire a SALVO (fanned a few degrees apart). A map shows your launchers, the target and your running torpedoes.
- Torpedo T2 now HOMES: it listens ahead for the loudest ship and turns for it; when it hears nothing it pings to find a quiet sub close by. Torpedo T3 is WIRE-GUIDED: it steers for the target picked on the fire control console (and follows it when you change it) for 450 blocks, then homes like a T2.
- Torpedo T4 (supercavitating): a rocket torpedo in its own gas bubble, 80 blocks/s for 1,000 blocks with the biggest warhead: a hit implodes a submarine at any depth. It sprints to the target picked on the fire control console, slows down near it, pings once and locks the nearest ship, then sprints in for the kill. A noisemaker does not fool it; turning hard while it searches might. Everyone on sonar hears it coming.
- New underwater explosions for torpedoes and depth charges, instead of the Minecraft TNT explosion: a flash and a shock ring in the water, a gas bubble that swells and collapses, and at the surface a white dome and a tall water column (shallow), a smaller late column (deeper) or only a boil of foam (deep). Under water you hear a heavy thump; above water a duller boom and the splash. Ship hits no longer make the TNT bang or puff.
- Underwater Drone (UUV): fire it from a torpedo tube like a torpedo. It runs 1,500 blocks quietly, then holds station and listens for 10 minutes on the tube's frequency, so your sonar console gets an ear far from the boat.
- Both are made on the torpedo line (pick them with the type buttons).
- Noisemaker: a decoy you use aboard a sub (it comes out under the hull) or in the water. For 30 seconds it is louder than any ship, so a homing torpedo turns for it.
- Depth charges and the Depth Charge Rack (1 x 2 x 3 stern rack): set the depth, then drop one or a pattern of three. They sink fast and go off at that depth, or when they hit a hull or the seabed. You can also throw one over the side by hand.
- Vertical Launch Cell (2 x 7 x 2, built down through the deck and hull) and the Anti-Sub Rocket: set the cell to your fire control console's frequency, pick a target on the console and press LAUNCH. The rocket is thrown out of the water, flies to the target and drops a homing torpedo there. Works surfaced or down to 40 blocks deep.
- SLBM (sub-launched ballistic missile): a new Build mode in the missile assembly bay (a T3 airframe, a T3 motor, guidance and any ground warhead, nukes included). It flies 8,000 blocks (a little less with a heavy warhead). Load it into a Vertical Launch Cell, type the strike target on the cell's screen and LAUNCH: it is thrown up out of the sea, the nose cap pops, the motor lights and it flies on as a normal missile, so radars, interceptors and the far view all see it.
- Cruise missiles fit the Vertical Launch Cell too: each launch uses a Cruise Capsule (new item, carries the missile's fuel; the cell holds 4). The capsule floats the missile up, breaks the surface, the dome blows off and the missile flies straight to the strike target; the empty capsule falls back and sinks.
- The Vertical Launch Cell holds one round (anti-sub rocket, SLBM or cruise missile) and shows what is loaded and its range. The fire control console's FIRE / SALVO also fire SLBM and cruise cells (at the cell's strike target, or the console's target when none is set).
- Launch control aboard: the Torpedo Fire Control Console now also lists the Coastal Batteries on its frequency (loaded canisters and status); FIRE on a battery row launches one missile and SALVO includes them. Its new STRIKE page sets a strike target (type X and Z, SET) for every SLBM and cruise cell on the frequency that has no target of its own, and tells you how many cells can reach it; it shows each cell's and battery's distance, range and where it aims, can give the selected cell or battery its own target, and STRIKE SALVO fires every ready cell and battery. A deep submarine without its comms buoy still only takes orders from its own consoles.
- Sonobuoy: use it on your sonar console to copy its frequency, then throw it on the sea. It floats, lowers a hydrophone and listens for 10 minutes, so two buoys (or a buoy and a dome) can find a sub's position.
- Torpedo tubes and deck launchers have a GYRO setting: the torpedo runs 10 blocks straight out of the tube, then turns up to 90 degrees left or right onto its course. They also get a frequency tab for the fire control console.
- The sonar console and the fire control console now show their pictures on their own screens in the world (the sonar map and history; the fire control map and firing solution), not just in the GUI.
- The sonar console now shows a MAP: your domes and arrays, a dashed line for every sound one sensor hears, a numbered spot where two sensors cross and a lettered spot for every ping result. The contacts are listed in plain words ("Engine, 44 blocks SE, at 184 128"); hover one for the details. The old waterfall is on the HISTORY tab.
- New sounds for all the deep-sea gear, in place of the Minecraft stand-ins: the sonar ping and sonar room hum, the fire control console and its firing key, the torpedo tube firing and reloading, the rack hoisting a torpedo, the deck launcher turning, torpedoes you can hear running (the T4 roars, the underwater drone whirs), the seeker ping, the noisemaker, sonobuoy and depth charge splashes and releases, the launch cell hatch and cold launch, the anti-sub rocket igniting and dropping its torpedo, and the torpedo line rolling, fitting and testing. The underwater blast now thumps under water, booms dully from above, and you hear the water column crash back or the sea boil; a hole torn in a hull gushes and an imploding submarine groans and bangs.

- Torpedo tiers now matter: T1, T2, T3 and T4 have warheads of growing power (8 / 9 / 10 / 12). A T1 hit can implode a submarine only deeper than 40 blocks, a T2 deeper than 30, a T3 deeper than 20, and the T4 at any depth (a sure kill if it hits); shallower, they tear a hole in it.
- Torpedoes and depth charges now blow craters in the world: 10 blocks across for a T1 up to 14 for a T4 (8 for a depth charge); they break the seabed, walls and piers, under water the hole fills with sea (no air pocket), and a big hit can bring down a block-built sea platform. A torpedo that strikes the seabed or a wall now gets the full underwater explosion.
- A holed ship under water no longer pours smoke: it leaks bubbles; only the part above the water smokes and burns.
- Torpedoes, the underwater drone and the anti-sub rocket keep the area they travel through loaded, so they keep going when they leave the area around you (server option `chunkLoadingForImpacts`).
- Noisemaker: 30 seconds between uses (one decoy in the water at a time).
- Torpedo tubes, deck launchers and racks now say they need "a torpedo (any type) or UUV" instead of "Torpedo T1".
- Fire control console: click a target on the map to pick it (the arrows still work). Launch cells on its frequency are now listed too: FIRE on a selected cell launches its rocket, SALVO launches every ready cell along with the tubes. "Aim all" without a target now tells you that SALVO fires without one.
- Ships far from every player (unloaded) can now be attacked: a sonar ping finds them where they stopped, and a torpedo or anti-sub rocket on its way loads the area ahead of it, so the ship is there when it arrives.
- Anti-sub rocket launch redone: it bursts out of the sea in a column of spray, hangs for a moment, ignites with a flash and a cloud of steam, turns over onto its course trailing thick white smoke, then the booster burns out and tumbles into the sea while the torpedo comes down under a small parachute about 80 blocks short of the target, pointing at it, and starts hunting. No more spinning in flight.
- Sonar console: a Hunting switch next to PING ALL. While it is on, every dome on the frequency pings again as soon as it can, so your target stays marked while you shoot (each ping costs power, and everyone hears it).
- Torpedoes and depth charges now go off when they reach the actual hull of a ship, not its outline: a torpedo passing the empty corner of a ship lying at an angle no longer blows up harmlessly in open water.
- Torpedo line rebuilt: you now watch each torpedo being made for real. The crane takes a steel plate to the bender, the rolls turn it into a hull section, a welding torch runs along the seam with sparks and the section is lifted onto the trolley; robot arms grip and fit the battery, nose and tail, the crane brings the warhead, every joint is welded; the finished torpedo is lowered all the way into a longer test tank, lifted out dripping, painted in its colour over the rack and set down. A run takes twice as long at half the power per second (the same energy per torpedo).

  - A sonar ping shows mines as contacts (you can pick one as a torpedo target), and a torpedo or depth charge going off close to a mine sets it off too. Sneak with an empty hand to take your mine back.
  - Lay mines from a ship: use a mine at the rail of a ship (or from a pier or a boat) and it goes over the side, splashes, sinks to the seabed and moors itself, its cable paying out until the mine floats at its depth. Sneak + use the mine in your hand to set that depth (3 to 90 blocks). A Rising Bottom Mine or a Captor Mine takes its torpedo from your inventory on the way down, so it lands loaded. You need at least 8 blocks of water, and you have to stand at the rail (not over your own deck). If it can't moor where it lands (land, someone's claim, no room), you get it back.

Fire it from its screen, with a redstone pulse, or with SALVO on a fire control console on its frequency. You see the drum fly off its arbor, the barrel kick back in a puff of smoke, and the davit swing the spare drum onto the barrel. A depth charge rack placed against it keeps it supplied. The depth charge leaves on its arbor (the cradle it sits on): the two part in the air and the arbor tumbles into the sea with its own splash; when the K-gun reloads, a fresh arbor drops into the barrel and the davit sets the next charge on it.

- Towed Array Winch (2 x 2 x 2): put it in your submarine's stern with the fairlead facing back. Powered, it streams a long sonar array behind the boat that follows your wake and hears ships 3,000 blocks away. It hears nothing while it streams out, while you go fast or for a while after a hard turn: run straight and slow to listen.
- Listening Node (2 x 3 x 2): a wireless sonar post for the seabed or a pier. No cables: it reports to your sonar consoles on its frequency and runs on its own battery; its little current turbine recharges it in water (for ever in flowing water).
- Coastal Battery (4 x 3 x 4): a shore anti-ship launcher with four cruise missiles. It turns to the target your fire control console picked (or a typed X Z), raises its canisters, blows the covers and fires missiles that skim the sea and home on the ship. Submerged submarines can't be hit.
- Dock Crane (3 x 6 x 3): loads your submarine at the pen. Put torpedoes or missiles in a chest or torpedo rack on the pier within the crane's reach and it swings, lowers its hook and loads them into torpedo tubes, launch cells, the coastal battery or the submarine's own torpedo rack.
- Manganese nodules: fields of metal lumps on the floor of deep oceans (new chunks only). You cannot mine them by hand: bring them up with a Dredge Winch or a Seabed Crawler. Turn them off with the `disabledOreGen` option ("nodules").
- Dredge Winch (2 x 3 x 2): place it on a pier or a ship looking out over the water. Its A-frame swings out and drops a grab bucket on a cable to the seabed; it grabs a nodule field within a few blocks and brings up 2-3 nodules. Slow and cheap: the early way. AUTO repeats it, DROP ONE or a redstone pulse does one.
- Collector Rig (5 x 5 x 5) + Seabed Crawler: stand the rig on the edge of a deck or pier, put a crawler in and press LAUNCH. The crawler goes down on its riser hose, finds the nodule fields within 24 blocks and drives from one to the next collecting them; the nodules are pumped up into the rig, which separates them into manganese and nickel-cobalt concentrate. RECALL brings the crawler home.
- Nodules smelt into manganese; the rig's concentrates give more manganese plus nickel-cobalt alloy. The T4 supercavitating torpedo now needs 2 nickel-cobalt alloy (instead of 2 titanium), and the crawler's tracks need manganese.
- Nodule survey: the Sonar Console has a new SEABED tab. Press SURVEY SEABED and every sonar dome on its frequency pings the seabed and maps the
  manganese nodule fields within 256 blocks: they show on the console's map at their real size and in a list (how many nodule blocks are left,
  how far, which way, how deep). A survey costs as much as a ping, shares its cooldown and every enemy sonar hears it. The marks stay for

- Naval Reactor (3 x 3 x 3): a compact submarine power plant, 50,000 FE/s at full power. Its glowing core sits in a round glass coolant tank that you can watch fill up: the sea it touches (a submerged sub fills it for free) or water piped in. Under a quarter full it starts to overheat. - Hull-integrated tops: the Periscope's top, the Escape Pod's and the Communications Buoy's hatch plate and the Towed Array Winch's back face are now part of your submarine's hull: build the hull with a hole and the block fills it.
